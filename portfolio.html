eCFR Analyzer

February 2025

https://github.com/JohnDDuncanIII/ecfr-analyzer

https://ecfr-analyzer-chi.vercel.app/

https://www.ecfr.gov/

On February 10th 2025, over the course of a 24-hour period, I wrote an Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (eCFR) Analyzer which:

  • Scrapes titles, agencies, and related CFR references from the eCFR into a PostgreSQL database.

  • Parses out the truncated text subset — title, subtitle, chapter, subchapter, part, subpart, or section — from the full title xml associated with an agency and its children (as specified in the eCFR API's agencies.json CFR references) and saves it to the associated CFR reference.

  • Calculates the total agency wordcounts from the truncated text subsets of their related CFR references.

  • Displays the information in three separate frontend views:

    • Agency Word Counts
    • Agency CFR Titles & Text
    • Historical Title Changes

  • Backend: Django REST API

  • Frontend: Next.js/React with TypeScript
    • Package Manager: Bun

    • Linting: Biome.js for Next.js/TypeScript
    • Component Library: shadcn
    • Style: tailwind
  • Deployment: Railway (backend) and Vercel (frontend)
  • Visualization: Highcharts

MessageFaces

March 2016

https://github.com/JohnDDuncanIII/messagefaces

http://cs.gettysburg.edu/~duncjo01/sites/messagefaces/

In March 2016, I ported and expanded MessageFaces—a legacy Mozilla Suite mailnews XUL/XPCOM extension which displays ‘face’ avatar images which are intended to represent the sender in the form of photos, headshots, cartoons, logos, picons, etc. and originally written by Jens Bannmann in 2004—to the latest version of SeaMonkey.

The extension had been broken for years due to changes in Mozilla's MailNews API, so I fixed the issues and restored compatibility with the latest versions of SeaMonkey and Thunderbird. However, support for Thunderbird was later deprecated and disabled following the removal of XUL overlays in Thunderbird 78.

MessageFaces displays an X-Face—a compressed 1-bit ascii image format originally written by James Ashton—or a Face—a base64 encoded png originally specified by Lars M. Ingebrigtsen—either of which can be embedded within the message header section of email or usenet newsgroup messages. Additionally, MessageFaces supports address book icons, gravatars, and Steve Kinzler's picons.

MessageFaces is a spiritual successor to Rob Pike and Dave Presotto's Vismon from Plan 9 from Bell Labs, Chris Liebman's xfaces, Rich Burridge's faces, Steve Kinzler's Picons, and NeXT Computer's NeXTMail.

Some of my contributions include:

  • multiple header photos
  • gravatar icons with caching
  • decodes X-Face to png instead of legacy bmp (from mnenhy)
  • local and online picon database with caching
  • custom mailnews column
  • Thunderbird 3+ address book contact photo
  • extensive performance improvements
  • complete internal infrastructure rewrite

You can create your own X-Face using Jeff Dairiki's Online X-Face Converter

Classic Macintosh Fonts

February 2016

https://github.com/JohnDDuncanIII/macfonts

In February 2016, I discovered a novel method to losslessly convert Susan Kare's original Macintosh fonts from their Classic Mac OS resource fork to a modern macOS (OS X) data fork.

In March 2016, I worked with archivists from the comp.sys.mac.vintage usenet newsgroup to track down and digitize rare and obscure Classic Mac OS ROMs in order to dump their fonts. This effort uncovered the Sony 6.9/7.0, Twiggy, and System 0.97 pre-release ROMs.

Received positive feedback from both Susan Kare and Chris Espinosa.

All of the screenshots below were taken in a live TextEdit.app window on macOS Sequoia.

Poker Squares

March 2015–Spring 2016

https://github.com/JohnDDuncanIII/pokersquares

https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=edu.gettysburg.pokersquares

From March 2015 to Spring 2016, I designed and wrote an Android application to play Poker Squares—a solitaire-based card game where the player attempts to create the best poker hand in vertical and horizontal rows and columns on a 5x5 grid—as side project for the Gettysburg club chapter of the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM).

PokerSquares uses an expectimax Native Abstract Reinforcement Learning (NARL) Artificial Intelligence opponent AI player created by my academic advisor Dr. Todd W. Neller.

Four color poker card deck adapted from Susan Kare's Windows 3.0 Solitaire playing cards set from 1988, as detailed in the Playing Cards portfolio entry below.

Published to the Google Play Store in September 2016 and has accrued over 1k+ downloads with an average rating of 5 stars.

Red Light Race

Fall 2013

https://github.com/JohnDDuncanIII/redlight

https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=edu.gettysburg.redlight&hl=en_US

In Fall 2013, I designed and wrote an Android application to play Red Light Race — a jeopardy chip/card game by Dr. Todd W. Neller and based on the Gamewright jeopardy card game Fowl Play which is, in turn, based on the folk jeopardy dice game Pig — for my freshman year cs112 course at Gettysburg College.

RedLightRace uses an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) opponent AI player created by my academic advisor Dr. Todd W. Neller and Marcin Malec (‘13) from Gettysburg College.

Published to the Google Play Store in May 2014 and has accrued over 1k+ downloads with an average rating of 5 stars.

I was featured on the Gettysburg College homepage.
